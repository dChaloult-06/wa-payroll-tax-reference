  • PFML premium increased from 0.92% (2025) to 1.13% effective January 1, 2026 — driven by higher program utilization.
  • SUI wage base increased from $72,800 (2025) to $78,200 effective January 1, 2026.

  • WA Cares Fund SB 5291 effective January 1, 2026 — expanded exemption categories including automatic exemption for temporary visa holders; opt-in rights for previously exempt employees; out-of-state coverage continuation after 3 years.

What Else Activates When You Register?

Registering with ESD for SUI triggers:

  • →EAF assessment on the same $78,200 wage base (appears on same tax rate notice)
  • →PFML reporting obligations through the ESD shared system
  • →WA Cares Fund collection and remittance (same quarterly ESD report as of July 2023)
  • →SOC code reporting requirement — filings rejected without valid codes

Registering with L&I for Workers' Comp triggers:

  • →Separate account from ESD — do not conflate L&I and ESD registrations
  • →Risk class assignment based on industry and job duties
  • →Hourly premium obligations beginning first day of employment
Important — Business Nexus Consideration: Registering for Washington payroll taxes when not mandatory creates a business connection (nexus) to Washington that may trigger other business tax obligations — including B&O tax through the Department of Revenue — even if the payroll registration itself is voluntary. Consult a qualified tax advisor before voluntarily registering in any new state to understand the full scope of obligations that may follow.

Reciprocity — Washington State

Washington has no personal income tax, which means it has no formal income tax reciprocity agreements with other states. However, this creates specific scenarios for multi-state employees that payroll professionals need to navigate carefully.

Why Washington has no reciprocity agreements: Reciprocity agreements allow residents of one state to be taxed only in their state of residence, not the state where they work. Because Washington imposes no personal income tax, there is nothing to reciprocate — WA residents working in another state owe no WA income tax regardless, and non-residents working in WA have no WA income tax liability either.

PFML & SUI Multi-State Sourcing — The 4-Part Test

For PFML and SUI, Washington follows the federal four-part test to determine which state has jurisdiction. Apply each test in order — stop at the first test that produces a definitive answer.

Test (Apply in Order)RuleWA Applies If…
1. LocalizationWhere is the work primarily performed?Employee's work is localized to WA
2. Base of OperationsWhere does the employee start/end their work?Employee's base is in WA
3. Direction & ControlWhere is the employer directing work from?Direction/control emanates from WA
4. Employee ResidenceWhere does the employee live?Employee resides in WA (tiebreaker)
Practical application: For a fully remote employee hired to work from their WA home, Test 1 (localization) produces a clear answer — WA PFML and SUI apply. For a traveling employee who splits time across states, Test 2 or 3 determines jurisdiction. Only one state collects SUI for any given employee.

Glossary

Key terms used throughout this reference. Definitions reflect how terms are applied in Washington payroll tax compliance contexts.

Wage Base
The maximum amount of an employee's annual wages subject to a specific tax. Once an employee's wages reach the wage base for the year, that tax stops being collected. Washington's SUI wage base is $78,200 in 2026; Social Security's is $184,500. WA Cares has no wage base — it applies to all gross wages.
Experience Rating
A method of setting employer SUI tax rates based on the employer's own history of unemployment claims. Employers with fewer claims generally receive lower rates; those with more claims pay higher rates within the statutory range (0.27%–6.02% in WA).
Premium vs. Tax
Washington's PFML and WA Cares assessments are technically "premiums" — payments into an insurance fund — rather than taxes. This distinction affects how they are treated for federal tax purposes and is the reason behind the HB 2345 reallocation between family and medical leave shares.
Nexus
A sufficient connection between a business and a state that creates a legal obligation to register, file, and pay taxes in that state. For payroll purposes, nexus is typically established when an employer has an employee working in the state.
Reciprocity Agreement
A formal agreement between two states allowing residents of each state to pay income tax only in their state of residence, regardless of where they work. Washington has no such agreements because it has no personal income tax.
Four-Part Localization Test
The standard test used to determine which state has SUI and PFML jurisdiction for an employee working in multiple states. Applied in order: (1) localization of work, (2) base of operations, (3) direction and control, (4) employee residence.
EAF (Employment Administration Fund)
A small Washington employer assessment added to the SUI rate, currently approximately 0.03%. It appears as a separate line on the ESD annual tax rate determination notice and shares the same $78,200 wage base as SUI.
Voluntary Contribution
An optional additional SUI payment an employer can make to the state to reduce their experience-rated tax rate for the following year. Washington permits voluntary contributions and they can be a cost-effective strategy for employers near a rate tier boundary.
SOC Code
Standard Occupational Classification code — a federal system for classifying workers by job type. Washington ESD requires a valid SOC code for every employee on quarterly wage reports. Filings submitted without valid SOC codes are rejected.
Supplemental Pension Fund (SPF)
A component of Washington's workers' compensation premium structure, set at $0.1912 per hour worked in 2026, split equally between employer ($0.0956) and employee ($0.0956). Separate from the Medical Aid Fund and other L&I components.
PFML Voluntary Plan
An employer-sponsored paid leave plan approved by ESD as an alternative to the state PFML program. Must provide benefits at least as generous as the state plan. Employers with approved voluntary plans do not remit to the state ESD fund.
JumpStart Payroll Expense Tax
Seattle's employer-paid payroll tax applying to businesses with annual payrolls of $9.07M or more, on employee compensation of $194,452 or more. Rates range from 0.746% to 2.557% based on payroll size tiers. An employer expense — not withheld from employees.
WA Cares Fund
Washington's mandatory, publicly funded long-term care insurance program — the first of its kind in the United States. Funded by a 0.58% employee payroll deduction on all gross wages with no cap. Benefits of up to $36,500 (inflation-adjusted) became available statewide July 1, 2026.
B&O Tax
Washington's Business & Occupation tax — a gross receipts tax on business revenue, administered by the WA Department of Revenue. Not a payroll tax and not withheld from employee wages, but relevant when analyzing the full cost of doing business in Washington and when evaluating voluntary payroll registration (which may trigger B&O review).

About This Tool

A practitioner-built reference for payroll professionals — source-linked, structured, and designed to bridge the gap between available information and accessible information.

What This Is — and Why It Exists

The statutes are public. The agency guidance is published. The employer guides exist. But for many payroll professionals, access to this information depends on what subscription their employer carries, what platform they work in, or whether someone with more experience happened to point them in the right direction.

This tool is designed to put that information where practitioners can actually reach it — organized the way a payroll tax professional thinks through a state: What is the obligation? What's the wage base and rate? Who pays it? What does registering trigger? Where do I go to verify it myself?

The Washington State edition started with a specific project: working on the software design for the Seattle JumpStart Payroll Expense Tax update that took effect in 2025. That work required a full pass through the Washington payroll tax landscape — the RCW sections, ESD employer guides, WA Cares Fund amendments, PFML rate changes, and nexus implications. The map was already being built. This tool is that map, structured for reuse.

This is the first in a planned series of state-specific payroll tax reference tools. Pennsylvania, Kentucky, and Ohio — states with layered local income tax structures — are next. Cross-state comparison guides on PFML programs, SUI wage bases, and reciprocity agreements are part of the roadmap.

Every rate in this tool is linked to its source. Every program card identifies the administering agency. The goal is not to replace the primary sources — it is to make them findable.
